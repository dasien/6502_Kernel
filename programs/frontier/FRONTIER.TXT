==============================================================================
                         F R O N T I E R   F O R T U N E
                     a trading game for the MFC 6502
==============================================================================

You step off the coach at Grisley Gultch with two thousand dollars, a wagon,
and fifty-five hundred owed to a man who does not forget.

You have sixty days.

Buy cheap, haul it somewhere they want it, sell dear. Pay the man before the
interest buries you, and walk away with as much as you can carry.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE GOAL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

At the end of day sixty -- or whenever you choose to retire -- your score is
what you are worth:

        cash + savings + cargo (at local prices) - debt

Cargo counts, so you are not punished for finishing with a full wagon. Debt
counts against you, so finishing rich and owing more is no victory at all.

The top eight fortunes are written into the Book of Fortunes and kept on the
disk. A negative score still gets a line -- somebody has to be the warning.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE GOODS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Eight things are worth hauling. Every town carries its own price for each,
and those prices move every day:

        Feed            $10  -  $59        Medicine     $500  -  $1,199
        Water           $20  -  $149       Guns       $1,000  -  $3,499
        Food            $30  -  $239       Lumber     $5,000  -  $12,499
        Whiskey        $300  -  $799       Gold      $15,000  -  $29,999

Your wagon holds one hundred units, and a unit of Feed takes exactly as much
room as a unit of Gold.

Read that twice. Space, not money, is what you are really short of. A wagon
full of Feed bought at its floor and sold at its ceiling earns you a few
thousand dollars; one bar of Gold in the same space can earn more than that by
itself. The whole game is the climb from hauling bulk to hauling value.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE TOWNS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Six towns, and they are not interchangeable. A town may be SHORT of a good --
it pays well for it -- or have a SURPLUS, where it goes cheap. It might have
both. It might have neither and be a perfectly ordinary place.

        v  (green, pointing down)     surplus here: buy
        ^  (red, pointing up)         short here:   sell

And it changes. Every week or two some town's fortunes shift: a shortage is
relieved, a new one opens up. The map is worth learning but it will not hold
still, and what you knew a month ago may be wrong.

You only learn a town by GOING there. The travel list shows what you found
last time you visited, and how long ago that was.

Your first journeys are exploration. After that they are a route -- one you
will have to keep checking.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MARKET IS ALIVE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Prices are not dice. Every town has a real, live price for every good at all
times, and it moves for reasons.

It drifts. Each day a price eases back toward what is normal for that town and
wanders a little on its own -- other people trade whether you are there or not.

AND IT ANSWERS TO YOU. Sell into a market and the price drops THE MOMENT you
sell. Not next visit -- you will watch it fall on the screen in front of you:

        sell  25 units of Water at $124  ->  the price is now $109
        sell  75 more                    ->  the price is now $69

A hundred units cuts a price roughly in half. Buying does the reverse: clear
out a town's cheap Feed and you have made it dear.

Then it heals, over about a week, back toward normal.

So no two towns are a machine you can run. Work one pair hard and the spread
closes in your hands -- buying dearer every trip, selling cheaper -- until the
route costs you money. Six towns exist so you can rotate and let one breathe.

Beside each price the board tells you how today compares with normal there:

        low      cheaper than this town usually asks
        high     dearer than usual


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
YOUR LEDGER
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Press L in town to open your ledger: every price you have personally seen, in
every town you have been to, with how many days ago you saw it.

This is the only place you can compare towns -- and it is a notebook, not a
telegraph. Those prices have drifted since you wrote them down, and a town's
needs shift every week or so too. An entry from yesterday is worth acting on.
An entry from three weeks ago is a rumour.

Which is the real reason to keep moving: information rots.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SCREENS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From the status screen:

        S       General Store -- buy and sell, and buy a sidearm
        T       Travel -- pick a town and spend a day getting there
        B       Bank -- put cash somewhere safe
        D       Loan Office -- pay the debt down, or borrow more
        W       Wagon Yard -- buy more space
        C       Casino -- Hi-Lo, for the desperate
        L       Ledger -- every price you have written down
        Q       Retire and settle up

ESC backs out of any screen, and cancels any prompt.

Every "how much?" prompt tells you your real maximum -- all you can afford when
buying, all you are carrying when selling -- so you will never type a number
and then be refused. And since "all of it" is what you usually want:

        ENTER (on an empty field)    fills in the maximum
        A                           the same thing
        ENTER again                  agrees to it

It fills rather than commits, so you always see the figure before it happens.
Type a smaller number instead if you would rather.


GENERAL STORE
    Press 1-8 to choose a good, then B to buy or S to sell.
    ENTER twice buys all you can afford, or sells everything you have.
    Press G to buy a SIDEARM for $250. See ROAD AGENTS below.
    You buy with CASH. Money in the bank must be withdrawn first.

TRAVEL
    Press 1-6. One day passes, the debt grows, and every market moves on.

BANK
    Deposits are safe from anything that happens on the trail. They earn no
    interest -- this is a strongbox, not an investment. The catch is that
    banked money cannot be spent until you come back and withdraw it.

LOAN OFFICE
    P pays the debt down. B borrows more, up to $100,000 total.
    Borrowing is real capital now against real interest later. Whether that
    trade is ever worth taking is the question the game is asking you.

WAGON YARD
    $500 buys another wagon and twenty more units of space.

CASINO
    Bet, then call High or Low against the dealer's card. Ties push.
    It is an even bet -- no house edge, and no way to grind it for income.
    What it can do is turn a bad run into a wild one.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE DEBT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every day you spend on the trail, the debt grows by about two percent.

Left completely alone, fifty-five hundred dollars becomes roughly eighteen
thousand by day sixty. It compounds, so it is cheapest to deal with early and
most expensive to ignore. The loan office shows you exactly what the next day
will cost.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE TRAIL
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

About one journey in five, something happens:

    - a hidden shortcut, and the day costs you nothing
    - a washed-out bridge, and it costs you two
    - word of a shortage, and one good's price here doubles
    - a glut, and one good's price here is cut in half
    - a new settlement, and EVERYTHING here is in demand
    - a cache of goods by the roadside, free for the loading
    - a swollen river, and some of your load goes downstream


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ROAD AGENTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

About one journey in eight, riders block the trail.

They are not fools. They go for whatever is worth most -- your purse if it is
fat, your most valuable cargo if it is not.

    UNARMED, you can only watch it happen.

    ARMED, you get a choice: FIGHT or HAND IT OVER.
    Fight and win, and they scatter with nothing -- a little better than even
    odds. Fight and lose, and they take twice as much AND your pistol.

That is the whole point of the $250 sidearm. It does not make you safer by
sitting in your holster. It buys you a decision at the moment one is worth
having.

One thing they can never touch: MONEY IN THE BANK. That is what the bank is
for, and it is why leaving profits in your pocket is a choice with a cost.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADVICE FROM MEN WHO DID NOT MAKE IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Space is the scarce thing, not dollars. Ask what a unit of room is earning.

Learn the map early, while the debt is small -- then check it again, because
towns change their minds.

Watch what your selling does to a price. If it sags as you unload, the wagon
was too full for this town.

Deal with the shark early. Two percent of a small debt is nothing. Two percent
of a large one, sixty times over, is the whole game.

Bank what you cannot afford to lose, before the journey and not after.

Buy the sidearm. It is a quarter of one percent of what a full wagon of Gold
is worth, and the men on the road know the difference.
